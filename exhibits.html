The Local BeehiveHoney bees that pollinate the garden center next door▼

Bees at work

Behind the glass of our observation hive, thousands of honey bees live as one team. Each day they fly out to the garden center beside the zoo, visit flowers and carry pollen from bloom to bloom. That helps the plants make seeds and fruit, and gives the bees nectar to bring home. Look for a bee with bright pollen packed on her back legs.

  • The queen is the only bee that lays eggs, sometimes over a thousand in a day.
  • Workers are all female. As they grow up they clean, nurse babies, build wax, guard the door and finally collect food.
  • Drones are the males. They do not sting or collect food.
Did you know? The waggle dance

A bee that finds great flowers returns and dances. The angle of the dance points toward the flowers, and a longer waggle means they are farther away.

Did you know? Hexagon homes

Six-sided cells fit together with no gaps, so bees use the least wax to store the most honey.

Leaf Cutter DisplayWatch the colony at work throughout the year▼

An ant farm with a secret

Leaf cutter ants do not eat the leaves they carry. They march them home, chew them into mulch and use it to grow a fungus garden. The fungus is their real food. Watch the colony work all year: trails of ants waving green "flags," the garden and the busy tunnels.

  • Foragers cut leaves with jaws that vibrate like tiny saws and carry loads many times their own weight.
  • Tiny gardeners tend the fungus. Some ride on leaf pieces to guard carriers from parasite flies.
  • Soldiers and the queen defend the nest and lay the eggs. Wild colonies can reach millions of ants.
Did you know? The trash pile

Ants keep a separate dump for old waste so the garden stays healthy. Can you spot it?

Did you know? Why they matter

In the wild these ants recycle huge amounts of plant material and mix it into the soil, which helps other plants grow.

Creepy Crawly CaveUse a blacklight to light up our scorpions and spiders▼

Glow in the dark

In normal light our scorpions and spiders look dull. Switch on the blacklight and a scorpion glows blue-green. Try it with the button, then try it for real in the cave.

  • Scorpions have pincers and a stinger on a curled tail. They hunt at night, and mothers carry babies on their backs.
  • Spiders make silk. Some spin webs, but many hunt without one.
  • Not insects! Insects have six legs. Scorpions and spiders have eight, which makes them arachnids.
Did you know? Why scorpions glow

A substance in their outer shell soaks up invisible UV light and gives it back as visible blue-green light. Scientists are still learning why.

Did you know? Staying safe

Our animals stay behind glass. In nature most are shy, so if you find one outside, leave it alone and ask a grown-up.
