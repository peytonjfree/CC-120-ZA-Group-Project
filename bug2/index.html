Leaf Cutter Display

Watch a whole ant colony at work, all year long.

The big idea

Leaf cutter ants do not eat the leaves they carry. They march them home, chew them into mulch and use it to grow a fungus garden. The fungus is their real food. Our display lets you see the whole farm: trails of ants waving green "flags," the fungus garden and the busy tunnels. A colony never takes a day off, so every visit shows something new.

Meet the crew

Foragers

These ants cut leaf pieces with sharp jaws that vibrate like tiny saws. They can carry loads many times heavier than themselves.

Tiny gardeners

The smallest workers tend the fungus and keep it clean. Some even ride on leaf pieces to guard the carriers from little parasite flies.

Soldiers and queen

Big-headed soldiers defend the nest. Usually just one queen lays all the eggs, and wild colonies can grow to millions of ants.

What to look for

The leaf highway

Ants follow scent trails, so they travel in neat lines. Watch for ants going out empty and coming back with leaf pieces.

The fungus garden

The pale, fluffy sponge is the fungus. Ants feed it leaf mulch, and it makes food the ants and their babies can eat. Farming like this has gone on for millions of years.

The trash pile

Ants keep a separate dump for old waste so the garden stays healthy. Can you spot it?

Why do these ants matter?

In the wild they recycle huge amounts of plant material and mix it into the soil, which helps other plants grow.
